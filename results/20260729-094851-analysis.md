# Upload scenario after the streaming multipart rewrite

Two runs of 2026-07-29, `upload` only, five rounds each, interleaved, four
workers, 8s per sample with a 2s warmup:

- 8 connections — [`20260729-093834-run.log`](20260729-093834-run.log),
  [summary](20260729-093834-summary.txt),
  [analysis](20260729-093834-analysis.txt)
- 32 connections — [`20260729-094851-run.log`](20260729-094851-run.log),
  [summary](20260729-094851-summary.txt),
  [analysis](20260729-094851-analysis.txt)

The equivalence gate ran before each and passed: **120/120 checks agree across
blazingly, axum, actix, tokio, fastapi**, both times.

The change under test is confined to `rust/blazingly-api`: the cover-upload
handler now reads the request body as a stream
(`UploadBody::into_multipart()`), counting each chunk and dropping it, where it
previously took `File<UploadFile>` and received the whole part already
materialized. No other endpoint moved, and the success response is byte-identical
— 102 bytes, `{"id":1,"cover_url":"…","bytes":5242880,"content_type":"image/jpeg"}`
— as are the statuses of every error path the endpoint can produce.

## Read this run for memory, not for speed

The host was at **85.6% background CPU** before both runs and 85.9% after the
second. The README's own rule is that anything above roughly 20% is directional
at best; this is four times that. The previous upload runs
(`20260729-021856`, `20260729-022650`) were taken at 20.5% and 22.3%, so the
before/after comparison below is between a quiet host and a loaded one.

That ruins the throughput half of this run and leaves the memory half intact,
for a reason visible in the data rather than assumed: **the four implementations
that did not change reproduce their earlier peak RSS to within 5%**, while their
throughput fell by a factor of two to four. Resident memory on this scenario is
a property of how the body is handled; throughput today is a property of what
else the machine was doing.

| impl | peak RSS 8c, quiet host | peak RSS 8c, this run | drift | rps 8c, quiet | rps 8c, this run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| axum | 33.5 MiB | 34.4 MiB | +2.7% | 652 | 197 |
| actix | 32.2 MiB | 33.9 MiB | +5.3% | 669 | 296 |
| tokio | 36.0 MiB | 36.6 MiB | +1.7% | 650 | 287 |
| fastapi | 315.0 MiB | 321.4 MiB | +2.0% | 64 | 27 |

Those four are the control. They make the memory column below trustworthy and
the throughput column below unusable.

## Peak RSS: blazingly no longer scales with in-flight requests

Median of five rounds, as `tools/analyze_run.py` reports it.

| impl | 8 conn | 32 conn | 8 → 32 | 8 conn before | 32 conn before | before 8 → 32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **blazingly** | **25.9 MiB** | **25.9 MiB** | **1.00x** | 106.6 MiB | 300.4 MiB | 2.82x |
| axum | 34.4 MiB | 66.0 MiB | 1.92x | 33.5 MiB | 65.8 MiB | 1.96x |
| actix | 33.9 MiB | 49.1 MiB | 1.45x | 32.2 MiB | 47.5 MiB | 1.48x |
| tokio | 36.6 MiB | 76.5 MiB | 2.09x | 36.0 MiB | 76.2 MiB | 2.12x |
| fastapi | 321.4 MiB | 352.9 MiB | 1.10x | 315.0 MiB | 344.9 MiB | 1.09x |

Every individual sample, not just the medians:

```
 8 conn  blazingly  25.9, 25.9, 25.9, 25.9, 25.8
32 conn  blazingly  26.1, 25.9, 25.8, 26.1, 25.8
```

Ten samples spanning a fourfold rise in concurrency, all inside 25.8–26.1 MiB —
a 1.01x spread. **Blazingly's upload memory no longer scales with in-flight
requests.** It is flat.

The comparison that matters is against itself: 106.6 → 25.9 MiB at 8
connections (4.1x lower) and 300.4 → 25.9 MiB at 32 (11.6x lower). The buffered
handler grew 2.82x between the two connection counts; the streaming one grows
1.00x.

### Where it now stands against the others

At both connection counts blazingly has the **lowest peak RSS of all five
implementations on this scenario**, and it is the only one of the five whose
figure does not move with concurrency:

- at 8 connections: 25.9 MiB against actix 33.9, axum 34.4, tokio 36.6 — 1.31x
  to 1.41x below them
- at 32 connections: 25.9 MiB against actix 49.1, axum 66.0, tokio 76.5 — 1.90x
  to 2.95x below them

The gap widens with concurrency because theirs grows and its does not. Sample
ranges do not overlap anywhere: blazingly's worst sample (26.1 MiB) is below
actix's best (33.1 MiB at 8 conn, 48.1 at 32 conn). This is the one comparison
in this run that clears both tests.

Note that axum, actix and tokio all stream too — they were never buffering the
whole body. What they hold and blazingly no longer does is a larger working set
per connection; the reader here carries one transport chunk plus a delimiter of
look-ahead, and that quantity does not depend on how many uploads are in flight.

## Throughput: unmeasured on this host

`tools/analyze_run.py` returns the same verdict for both runs:

| | rust-group gap | noise band | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 conn | 1.89x (actix over blazingly) | 1.75x – 4.42x | **inside the noise — unmeasured** |
| 32 conn | 1.94x (actix over tokio) | 1.25x – 10.83x | **inside the noise — unmeasured** |

Single-implementation spreads reached 4.42x (tokio, 8 conn) and 10.83x (tokio,
32 conn). One implementation measured five times disagreed with itself by more
than any two implementations disagreed with each other. Nothing about relative
throughput can be read out of this run.

**In particular, this run does not show that the streaming rewrite helped or
hurt upload throughput, and it must not be reported as if it did.** The last
trustworthy measurement is the pre-change pair on the quiet host, where the
buffered handler was genuinely behind — actix over blazingly 1.90x at 8
connections and tokio over blazingly 1.41x at 32, both with non-overlapping
ranges. Whether that gap survived the rewrite is an open question that needs a
re-run on an idle machine.

Per-implementation CPU% fell alongside throughput (blazingly 334% → 222% at 8
conn, axum 372% → 194%, actix 374% → 251%, tokio 370% → 273%), which is the
same host-saturation signal from a second angle rather than independent
evidence about any implementation.

## Sample hygiene

Six samples were discarded across the two runs, all for connection errors and
**none for status mismatches** — no implementation answered wrongly at any point.

- 8 conn: fastapi rounds 1, 2, 3, 5 (2, 3, 1 and 5 connection errors), leaving
  fastapi at n=1. Its figure there is a single sample and is reported as such.
- 32 conn: actix round 5 (24 errors), fastapi round 5 (1 error).

Every blazingly, axum and tokio sample completed clean at both connection counts.

The harness itself needed one fix to produce these runs. `Stop-Server` called
`taskkill` between samples, and `taskkill` writes to stderr whenever a member of
the process tree has already exited; under `$ErrorActionPreference = "Stop"`
that write became a terminating error inside a `finally` block and aborted the
whole matrix after round 1, with four rounds unmeasured and nothing recorded as
discarded. It now tolerates the stderr write, verifies the process actually
exited, and warns if one survives — a survivor would hold the port and let the
next sample attribute one process's memory to another's load.

## What this supports

- **Blazingly's upload memory is flat in concurrency** — 1.00x across a
  fourfold rise, every sample within 25.8–26.1 MiB.
- **It fell 4.1x at 8 connections and 11.6x at 32** against the buffered handler
  measured by the same harness.
- **It is now the lowest of the five at both connection counts**, with no sample
  overlap against any other implementation.
- The four unchanged implementations reproduced their earlier memory within 5%,
  which is what licenses the three claims above on a host this loaded.

## What this does not support

- **Any throughput comparison at all**, between any two implementations, at
  either connection count. Both runs land inside the noise band.
- **Any claim that the rewrite changed upload throughput.** The before and after
  were taken on hosts four times apart in background load; the change in the
  throughput column is consistent with the host alone.
- Any read of the CPU% column as an implementation property today.
- Anything about `list`, `detail`, `filter`, `search` or `bulk`, which were not
  run here and whose handlers were not touched.
